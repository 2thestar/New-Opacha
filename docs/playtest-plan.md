# Campaign and balance playtest

The target experience is a short, readable territorial puzzle: choose where to commit, watch a capture land, then use that new foothold. Captures should feel rewarding because they change the position. Difficulty comes from timing, routes and reserves, rather than hidden bonuses or frantic clicking.

## Work and evidence

- Ship 48 fixed, editable maps, 12 each for conquest, neutral flag, starting flags and alliances. Use named tactical layouts and explicit objectives, not a list of random seeds.
- Arrange them into four teaching chapters. Introduce troop orders, half sends, growth, shields, AI personalities, directional routes, construction, bombs and artillery before combining them with new victory rules. A new concept gets a short paused briefing; the next maps develop and test it.
- Keep the user's weapon prices, damage, travel multipliers and 70% alliance transfer rule. Measure weaknesses and recommend numerical changes separately, with evidence.
- Compare expansion, economy, rushing, siege, diplomacy, hesitant play and pause-assisted planning. Test players use only visible state, delayed decisions, limited orders and seeded mistakes. They are scripted approximations, not recruited human players.
- Record wins, simulated duration, first capture, captures and recaptures, longest capture drought, orders, upgrades, tools and pacts. Check starting-position swaps, multiple seeds, incomplete games, weak openings, route exploits and alliance-only victories.
- Validate every authored map for connectivity, legal geometry, objective ownership, matching counts, export/import and at least one tested winning line. Repeat with multiple approaches to avoid teaching one brittle sequence.
- Use real touch input and inspect desktop and phone views for campaign selection, briefs, first capture, each new tool, victory, replay and editor changes. Verify Android update installation and capture feedback.

Automated wins and timing can identify a dead end or a dominant approach. They cannot establish enjoyment, comprehension or physical haptic feel. A short blind phone playtest remains necessary: record the first confusing action, most satisfying capture, most frustrating loss and whether the player chooses another map.

## Balance questions

1. Can a slower player recover from a mistaken send without an immediate forced loss?
2. Does early expansion beat waiting everywhere? Can a reserve or an economic detour sometimes beat rushing?
3. Do directional routes create readable attacks and safe detours, rather than accidental traps?
4. Can digging open a useful flank without being a compulsory 80-troop tax?
5. Do bombs and artillery solve a position where ordinary troop movement is worse?
6. Do flag races reward preparation without a starting-position advantage deciding the result?
7. Does diplomacy require a useful choice, or can every player instantly ally and skip the map?
8. Is there a reasonable next capture during the middle and end of a match?

Use paired seeds and the same policies before and after a change. Report unfinished matches instead of silently omitting them. Retain raw, reproducible results beside the analysis.
